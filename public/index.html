Questions

How is the recommendation worked out?

Every server plan that fits your CPU and memory is combined with every object storage and CDN option. Each combination is priced for one month: the plan, traffic beyond what it includes (or the cloud's per-GB egress), storage, requests and CDN traffic. The cheapest overall, the cheapest near your visitors and the one with the fewest vendors are shown; the table lists the rest. A CDN is assumed to serve 80% from cache and fetch the rest from the origin, billed by the origin's own rules.

Why are the clouds so much more expensive?

AWS, Google Cloud and Azure bill every GB that leaves for the internet (about $0.08 to $0.12), while most VPS plans include 1 to 20 TB a month. At a few TB a month the traffic costs more than the server itself.

What does "static files" mean, and what should I enter?

Files that are the same for every visitor: images, downloads, video, scripts. They can be served by object storage or a CDN instead of your server. A blog is mostly static (around 80%), an API or proxy is not at all (0%), a download site almost entirely (95%).

When is object storage plus a CDN worth it?

When static files are most of the traffic and more than your server's plan includes. Cloudflare R2 charges nothing for egress, and Backblaze B2 is free behind Cloudflare or Bunny, so a few TB of downloads can cost a few dollars instead of hundreds.

Why is inbound traffic free and outbound billed?

Providers buy transit by the traffic leaving their network, and inbound traffic draws customers in. So uploads are free, and downloads to your visitors are what you pay for.

What are Google Cloud's Premium and Standard tiers?

Premium carries traffic on Google's own network to the edge nearest the visitor, priced by destination; Standard hands it to the public internet near the data center and costs less. Azure's "Microsoft network" and "Internet" routing preferences are the same idea.

Why does traffic to mainland China cost more?

International links into China are scarce and expensive. Google Cloud prices Premium tier traffic to China separately (around twice the normal rate), and routes into China are often slow whatever you pay. Asia-Pacific data centers are usually the closest.

Why is performance not compared?

This is a price and spec filter, not a benchmark. Shared-CPU plans (marked in the table) can slow down when neighbors are busy; turn them off under More options if that matters.

Is buying several small servers for their traffic worth it?

Sometimes several small plans include more traffic than one large one. The planner does not search for that; set the number of servers yourself under More options and compare.

How current are the prices?

Every price was checked by hand against the provider's pricing page; the date is in the footer, and each row links to its source. Prices exclude VAT. Euro prices use a fixed exchange rate. Found an error? Open an issue on GitHub.

How is latency measured, and what about routes into mainland China?

Your browser times a few small requests to each data center on the page, using the same endpoints and method as Me2Cloud: one warm-up, then the median of three. It is your own network's latency, not a lab figure. Routes into mainland China vary a lot by provider and carrier, and price says nothing about them: if your visitors are in China, run the page from there (or ask one of them to) and compare the Hong Kong and Tokyo numbers.
